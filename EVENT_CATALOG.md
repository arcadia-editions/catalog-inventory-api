# Catalog Inventory

## Overview

Catalog Inventory is the stewardship capability for the scarce, physical side
of Arcadia Editions releases. It maintains a reliable business view of the
copies associated with an offering and protects the integrity of limited-edition
availability. Its purpose is to ensure that stock is represented thoughtfully
throughout its operational life, from being brought into the estate to being set
aside, released, sold, or corrected.

This page explains the enduring business role of Catalog Inventory. It is a
publication-oriented description, not a substitute for the repository's formal
artifacts. Those artifacts provide the detailed information needed by teams
working with the service, while this page focuses on the language, scope, and
value of the capability itself.

## Domain context

Catalog Inventory belongs to the Inventory Management subdomain of the Catalog
& Inventory domain. Inventory Management addresses the controlled availability
of editions whose quantity matters to the business and to collectors. In this
context, each unit carries commercial significance: availability must be
represented with care so that the catalogue remains credible and the promise of
a limited release is respected.

The subdomain distinguishes the commercial definition of a release from the
operational position of its copies. Catalog Inventory concentrates on the latter.
It provides the disciplined view required to understand whether units are
available for use, temporarily committed, no longer available, or in need of
backoffice attention. This creates a stable vocabulary for inventory decisions
without conflating them with the wider presentation of the catalogue.

## Responsibilities

Catalog Inventory maintains inventory positions for catalogue items and manages
the lifecycle of scarce stock claims. It records the business effects of stock
being received, adjusted, reserved, released, and sold so that the current
position is meaningful and traceable. It also supports the careful handling of
temporary commitments, ensuring that a copy can be held when appropriate and
made available again when that commitment no longer applies.

The service supports backoffice stewardship as well as controlled allocation.
Inventory teams can use its concepts to reflect stock intake and corrections,
keeping the recorded position aligned with the physical reality of an edition.
Its responsibilities are designed around consistency: an edition's scarcity
should be understood from a single authoritative operational perspective.

## Boundaries

Catalog Inventory owns stock positions and the business rules around reserving
and releasing scarce copies. It does not define the commercial identity,
editorial description, pricing, or publication status of a product. Nor does it
own payment decisions, customer communications, transport activity, or the
physical warehouse processes used to carry out a stock movement.

The service is also not a general asset register. Its boundary is the inventory
meaning required for catalogue releases: quantities, commitments, and the
operational status of available copies. Keeping this focus prevents accounting
of scarcity from being diluted by concerns that belong to other business
capabilities.

## Business value

Catalog Inventory protects confidence in Arcadia Editions' limited releases. A
clear and authoritative view of stock helps the organisation uphold the value of
scarcity, make responsible commitments, and avoid presenting availability that
cannot be honoured. It gives backoffice teams a dependable basis for managing
exceptions and maintaining an accurate picture of the estate.

By separating inventory stewardship from catalogue definition, the service also
makes change easier to reason about. Teams can improve stock practices without
altering the commercial story of a release, while catalogue work can remain
focused on the offer itself. The result is a more resilient operational
foundation for a curated, limited-edition business.
